Cicero Quotes

Cicero (Statesman and orator, 106 BC – 43 BC). Below are 31 quotes by Cicero from QuotePlace, each as a quote card.

Statesman and orator · 106 BC – 43 BC

Marcus Tullius Cicero was a Roman statesman, lawyer and orator considered the master of Latin prose. His speeches, letters and philosophical works, including On Friendship, transmitted Greek philosophy to the Roman world.

Quotes by Cicero

1.Friendship improves happiness and abates misery, by the doubling of our joy and the dividing of our grief.
2.Gratitude is not only the greatest of virtues, but the parent of all the others.
3.We must not say every mistake is a foolish one.
4.We are not born for ourselves alone.
5.While there's life, there's hope.
6.Not for ourselves alone are we born.
7.Not to know what happened before you were born is to be a child forever.
8.The life of the dead is placed in the memory of the living.
9.The shifts of fortune test the reliability of friends.
10.Old age, especially when it has filled offices of high public trust, has so much authority, that for this alone it is worth all the pleasures of youth.
11.The safety of the people shall be the highest law.
12.For laws are silent among arms.
13.The greater the difficulty, the greater the glory.
14.Friendship is the only thing in the world concerning the usefulness of which all mankind are agreed.
15.For just as I approve of the young man in whom there is a touch of age, so I approve of the old man in whom there is some of the flavour of youth.
16.The life given us by nature is short; but the memory of a well-spent life is eternal.
17.We are slaves of the law in order that we may be free.
18.True law is right reason in agreement with nature.
19.It is the peculiar quality of a fool to perceive the faults of others, and to forget his own.
20.No one is so old as to think that he may not live a year.
21.What the object of senile avarice may be I cannot conceive. For can there be anything more absurd than to seek more journey money, the less there remains of the journey?
22.The foundation of justice, moreover, is good faith, that is, truth and fidelity to promises and agreements.
23.In anger nothing right or judicious can be done.
24.There is no duty more obligatory than the repayment of kindness.
25.The authority of those who teach is often an obstacle to those who wish to learn.
26.It is not by muscle, speed, or physical dexterity that great things are achieved, but by reflection, force of character, and judgement.
27.Natural ability without education has more often raised a man to glory and virtue than education without natural ability.
28.A friend is, as it were, a second self.
29.If you have a garden and a library, you have everything you need.
30.Any man is liable to a mistake; but no one but a downright fool will persist in error.
31.The sinews of war are infinite money.
