Playwright and poet · 1564 – 1616

English playwright and poet, widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language; author of Hamlet and Macbeth.

Quotes by William Shakespeare

1.The fault, dear Brutus, is not in our stars, but in ourselves.
2.Brevity is the soul of wit.
3.Cowards die many times before their deaths; the valiant never taste of death but once.
4.Love all, trust a few, do wrong to none.
5.Some are born great, some achieve greatness, and some have greatness thrust upon them.
6.The course of true love never did run smooth.
7.All that glitters is not gold.
8.Love looks not with the eyes, but with the mind, and therefore is winged Cupid painted blind.
9.How far that little candle throws his beams! So shines a good deed in a weary world.
10.Having nothing, nothing can he lose.
11.He that is giddy thinks the world turns round.
12.Speak low, if you speak love.
13.Be great in act, as you have been in thought.
14.Be not afraid of greatness: some are born great, some achieve greatness, and some have greatness thrust upon them.
15.How far that little candle throws its beams! So shines a good deed in a naughty world.
16.God has given you one face, and you make yourself another.
17.Go to your bosom: Knock there, and ask your heart what it doth know.
18.We know what we are, but know not what we may be.
19.All the world is a stage, And all the men and women merely players.They have their exits and entrances; Each man in his time plays many parts.
20.To climb steep hills requires a slow pace at first.
21.It is not in the stars to hold our destiny but in ourselves.
22.There is nothing either good or bad, but thinking makes it so.
23.To be, or not to be: that is the question.
24.How far that little candle throws his beams! So shines a good deed in a naughty world.
25.What's in a name? That which we call a rose by any other name would smell as sweet.
26.This above all: to thine own self be true.
27.Parting is such sweet sorrow.
28.Our doubts are traitors, and make us lose the good we oft might win, by fearing to attempt.
29.Neither a borrower nor a lender be.
30.There are more things in heaven and earth, Horatio, than are dreamt of in your philosophy.
31.Love is merely a madness.
32.When we are born, we cry that we are come to this great stage of fools.
33.Better a witty fool than a foolish wit.
34.Hell is empty and all the devils are here.
35.We are such stuff as dreams are made on, and our little life is rounded with a sleep.
36.Sweet are the uses of adversity.
37.All the world's a stage, and all the men and women merely players.
38.Better three hours too soon than a minute too late.
39.Cowards die many times before their deaths.
40.Love is not love which alters when it alteration finds.
41.Give every man thy ear, but few thy voice.
42.The better part of valour is discretion.
43.One touch of nature makes the whole world kin.
44.My bounty is as boundless as the sea, my love as deep.
45.We are such stuff as dreams are made on.
46.The fool doth think he is wise, but the wise man knows himself to be a fool.
47.There is no darkness but ignorance.
48.Love is a smoke made with the fume of sighs.
49.Uneasy lies the head that wears a crown.
50.Modest doubt is called the beacon of the wise.
51.Our bodies are our gardens, to the which our wills are gardeners.
