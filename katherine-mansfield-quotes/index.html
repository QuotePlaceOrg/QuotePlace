Katherine Mansfield Quotes

Katherine Mansfield (Writer, 1888 – 1923). Below are 22 quotes by Katherine Mansfield from QuotePlace, each as a portrait card.

Writer · 1888 – 1923

New Zealand modernist short-story writer, a leading figure of early 20th-century literature alongside Joyce and Woolf.

Quotes by Katherine Mansfield

1.I want to be all that I am capable of becoming.
2.Everything in life that we really accept undergoes a change.
3.I am a writer first and a woman after.
4.Risk! Risk anything! Care no more for the opinion of others, for those voices. Do the hardest thing on earth for you. Act for yourself. Face the truth.
5.Make it a rule of life never to regret and never to look back. Regret is an appalling waste of energy; you can't build on it; it's only for wallowing in.
6.Risk! Risk anything! Care no more for the opinions of others.
7.The pleasure of all reading is doubled when one lives with another who shares the same books.
8.Could we change our attitude, we should not only see life differently, but life itself would come to be different.
9.The mind I love must have wild places, a tangled orchard where dark damsons drop in the heavy grass, an overgrown little wood, the chance of a snake or two, a pool that nobody's fathomed the depth of, and paths threaded with flowers planted by the mind.
10.It is of immense importance to learn to laugh at ourselves.
11.And after all the weather was ideal. They could not have had a more perfect day for a garden-party if they had ordered it.
12.Warm, eager, living life — to be rooted in life — to learn, to desire to know, to feel, to think, to act. That is what I want. And nothing less.
13.When we can begin to take our failures nonseriously, it means we are ceasing to be afraid of them.
14.If only one could tell true love from false love as one can tell mushrooms from toadstools.
15.It's an infernal nuisance to love Life as I do. I seem to love it more as time goes on rather than less. It never becomes a habit to me. It's always a marvel.
16.How hard it is to escape from places. However carefully one goes they hold you — you leave little bits of yourself fluttering on the fences — little rags and shreds of your very life.
17.To acknowledge the presence of fear is to give birth to failure.
18.I always felt that the great high privilege, relief and comfort of friendship was that one had to explain nothing.
19.Would you not like to try all sorts of lives — one is so very small — but that is the satisfaction of writing — one can impersonate so many people.
20.What can you do if you are thirty and, turning the corner of your own street, you are overcome, suddenly, by a feeling of bliss — absolute bliss! — as though you'd suddenly swallowed a bright piece of that late afternoon sun and it burned in your bosom, sending out a little shower of sparks into every particle, into every finger and toe?
21.Whenever I prepare for a journey I prepare as though for death. Should I never return, all is in order. This is what life has taught me.
22.Oh, how fascinating it was! How she enjoyed it! How she loved sitting here, watching it all!
