Elbert Hubbard Quotes

Elbert Hubbard (American writer and publisher, 1856 – 1915). Below are 25 quotes by Elbert Hubbard from QuotePlace, each as a text quote card.

American writer and publisher · 1856 – 1915

Elbert Hubbard was an American writer, publisher and philosopher who founded the Roycroft arts-and-crafts community. His essay "A Message to Garcia" (1899) was one of the most widely reprinted works of its era; he died in the sinking of the Lusitania.

Quotes by Elbert Hubbard

1.There is no failure except in no longer trying.
2.To avoid criticism, do nothing, say nothing, be nothing.
3.A little more persistence, a little more effort, and what seemed hopeless failure may turn to glorious success.
4.A failure is a man who has blundered but is not capable of cashing in on the experience.
5.The final proof of greatness lies in being able to endure criticism without resentment.
6.The greatest mistake you can make in life is to be continually fearing you will make one.
7.Do not take life too seriously. You will never get out of it alive.
8.It is only life and love that give love and life.
9.One machine can do the work of fifty ordinary men. No machine can do the work of one extraordinary man.
10.If put to the pinch, an ounce of loyalty is worth a pound of cleverness.
11.If you want work well done, select a busy man; the other kind has no time.
12.If you work for a man, in Heaven's name work for him.
13.The object of teaching a child is to enable him to get along without his teacher.
14.Never explain — your friends do not need it and your enemies will not believe you anyway.
15.Your friend is the man who knows all about you, and still likes you.
16.God will not look you over for medals, degrees or diplomas, but for scars.
17.Positive anything is better than negative nothing.
18.The best preparation for good work tomorrow is to do good work today.
19.The love we give away is the only love we keep.
20.Do your work with your whole heart, and you will succeed — there's so little competition.
21.The world is moving so fast these days that the man who says it can't be done is generally interrupted by someone doing it.
22.The line between failure and success is so fine that we scarcely know when we pass it — so fine that we are often on the line and do not know it.
23.It is not book-learning young men need, nor instruction about this and that, but a stiffening of the vertebrae which will cause them to be loyal to a trust, to act promptly, concentrate their energies: do the thing — 'Carry a message to Garcia.'
24.Art is not a thing; it is a way.
25.Every man is a damn fool for at least five minutes every day; wisdom consists in not exceeding the limit.
