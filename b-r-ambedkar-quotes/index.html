B.R. Ambedkar Quotes

B.R. Ambedkar (Social reformer, 1891 – 1956). Below are 38 quotes by B.R. Ambedkar from QuotePlace, each as a quote card.

Social reformer · 1891 – 1956

Indian jurist and social reformer who chaired the drafting of India's Constitution and campaigned against caste discrimination.

Quotes by B.R. Ambedkar

1.Cultivation of mind should be the ultimate aim of human existence.
2.Life should be great rather than long.
3.I measure the progress of a community by the degree of progress which women have achieved.
4.I like the religion that teaches liberty, equality and fraternity.
5.A great man is different from an eminent one in that he is ready to be the servant of society.
6.Be educated, be organised and be agitated.
7.So long as you do not achieve social liberty, whatever freedom is provided by the law is of no avail to you.
8.If I find the constitution being misused, I shall be the first to burn it.
9.Positively, my social philosophy may be said to be enshrined in three words: liberty, equality and fraternity.
10.History shows that where ethics and economics come in conflict, victory is always with economics.
11.Men are mortal. So are ideas. An idea needs propagation as much as a plant needs watering. Otherwise both will wither and die.
12.Caste is a notion; it is a state of the mind.
13.Constitutional morality is not a natural sentiment. It has to be cultivated.
14.Constitution is not a mere lawyers' document, it is a vehicle of Life, and its spirit is always the spirit of Age.
15.Political democracy cannot last unless there lies at the base of it social democracy.
16.An ideal society should be mobile, should be full of channels for conveying a change taking place in one part to other parts.
17.Democracy is not merely a form of government. It is primarily a mode of associated living, of conjoint communicated experience.
18.The Caste System is not merely a division of labour. It is also a division of labourers.
19.For a successful revolution it is not enough that there is discontent. What is required is a profound and thorough conviction of the justice, necessity and importance of political and social rights.
20.Political tyranny is nothing compared to social tyranny and a reformer who defies society is a much more courageous man than a politician who defies Government.
21.However good a Constitution may be, it is sure to turn out bad because those who are called to work it, happen to be a bad lot.
22.Caste has killed public spirit. Caste has destroyed the sense of public charity. Caste has made public opinion impossible.
23.If we wish to maintain democracy not merely in form, but also in fact, what must we do? The first thing in my judgement we must do is to hold fast to constitutional methods of achieving our social and economic objectives.
24.On the 26th of January 1950, we are going to enter into a life of contradictions. In politics we will have equality and in social and economic life we will have inequality.
25.I solemnly assure you that I will not die a Hindu.
26.Caste does not result in economic efficiency. Caste cannot improve, and has not improved, race. Caste has, however, done one thing. It has completely disorganised and demoralised the Hindus.
27.I prefer Buddhism because it gives three principles in combination, which no other religion does. Buddhism teaches prajna (understanding as against superstition and supernaturalism), karuna (love), and samata (equality). This is what man wants for a good and happy life. Neither god nor soul can save society.
28.In every country the intellectual class is the most influential class. This is the class which can foresee, advise and lead.
29.History bears out the proposition that political revolutions have always been preceded by social and religious revolutions.
30.Reason and morality are the two most powerful weapons in the armoury of a Reformer.
31.Makers of political constitutions must take account of social forces.
32.Caste in India means an artificial chopping off of the population into fixed and definite units, each one prevented from fusing into another through the custom of endogamy.
33.Religion is for man and not man for religion.
34.Slavery does not merely mean a legalized form of subjection. It means a state of society in which some men are forced to accept from others the purposes which control their conduct.
35.The freedom of mind is the real freedom. A person whose mind is not free, though he is not in chains, is a slave.
36.They cannot make history who forget history.
37.A just society is that society in which ascending sense of reverence and descending sense of contempt is dissolved into the creation of a compassionate society.
38.Unlike a drop of water which loses its identity when it joins the ocean, man does not lose his being in the society in which he lives. Man's life is independent.
