Henry Wadsworth Longfellow Quotes

Henry Wadsworth Longfellow (Poet, 1807 – 1882). Below are 10 quotes by Henry Wadsworth Longfellow from QuotePlace, each as a quote card.

Poet · 1807 – 1882

Henry Wadsworth Longfellow was an American poet and educator, among the most popular poets of his century. He wrote Paul Revere's Ride, The Song of Hiawatha and Evangeline, and produced the first American translation of Dante's Divine Comedy.

Quotes by Henry Wadsworth Longfellow

1.He that respects himself is safe from others; he wears a coat of mail that none can pierce.
2.Perseverance is a great element of success. If you only knock long enough and loud enough at the gate, you are sure to wake up somebody.
3.Into each life some rain must fall.
4.Life is real! Life is earnest! And the grave is not its goal.
5.Tell me not, in mournful numbers, life is but an empty dream.
6.Look not mournfully into the past, it comes not back again.
7.Learn to labor and to wait.
8.For after all, the best thing one can do when it is raining, is to let it rain.
9.We judge ourselves by what we feel capable of doing, while others judge us by what we have already done.
10.Music is the universal language of mankind — poetry their universal pastime and delight.
