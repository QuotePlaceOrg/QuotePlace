Jiddu Krishnamurti Quotes

Jiddu Krishnamurti (Philosopher, 1895 – 1986). Below are 34 quotes by Jiddu Krishnamurti from QuotePlace, each as a quote card.

Philosopher · 1895 – 1986

Indian philosopher and speaker on the nature of mind, meditation, and human freedom; rejected all guru roles.

Quotes by Jiddu Krishnamurti

1.The ability to observe without evaluating is the highest form of intelligence.
2.It is no measure of health to be well adjusted to a profoundly sick society.
3.To understand the immeasurable, the mind must be extraordinarily quiet, still.
4.Freedom from the desire for an answer is essential to the understanding of a problem.
5.The moment you have in your heart this extraordinary thing called love and feel the depth, the delight, the ecstasy of it, you will discover that for you the world is transformed.
6.Tradition becomes our security, and when the mind is secure it is in decay.
7.Truth is a pathless land.
8.One is never afraid of the unknown; one is afraid of the known coming to an end.
9.To be free of all authority, of your own and that of another, is to die to everything of yesterday, so that your mind is always fresh, always young, innocent, full of vigour and passion.
10.There is no end to education. It is not that you read a book, pass an examination, and finish with education. The whole of life, from the moment you are born to the moment you die, is a process of learning.
11.It is truth that liberates, not your effort to be free.
12.We all want to be famous people, and the moment we want to be something we are no longer free.
13.The constant assertion of belief is an indication of fear.
14.In oneself lies the whole world and if you know how to look and learn, the door is there and the key is in your hand. Nobody on earth can give you either the key or the door to open, except yourself.
15.The observer is the observed.
16.In the space which thought creates around itself there is no love.
17.You must understand the whole of life, not just one little part of it.
18.The ending of sorrow is the beginning of wisdom.
19.To ask the 'right' question is far more important than to receive the answer.
20.You can only be afraid of what you think you know.
21.Thought is so cunning, so clever, that it distorts everything for its own convenience.
22.All outward forms of change brought about by wars, revolutions, reformations, laws and ideologies have failed completely to change the basic nature of man and therefore of society.
23.To understand yourself is the beginning of wisdom.
24.When you call yourself an Indian or a Muslim or a Christian or a European, or anything else, you are being violent.
25.Meditation is not a means to an end. It is both the means and the end.
26.The transformation of the world is brought about by the transformation of oneself.
27.Without love the acquisition of knowledge only increases confusion and leads to self-destruction.
28.And as we are — the world is. That is, if we are greedy, envious, competitive, our society will be competitive, envious, greedy, which brings misery and war. The State is what we are.
29.The primary cause of disorder in ourselves is the seeking of reality promised by another.
30.Order imposed from without must always breed disorder.
31.Love is something that is new, fresh, alive. It has no yesterday and no tomorrow. It is beyond the turmoil of thought.
32.Intelligence is the capacity to perceive the essential, the what is; and to awaken this capacity, in oneself and in others, is education.
33.A mind that has merely been trained is the continuation of the past, and such a mind can never discover the new.
34.What lies beyond cannot be put into words because the word is not the thing.
