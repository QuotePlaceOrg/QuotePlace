Blaise Pascal Quotes

Blaise Pascal (Thinker, 1623 – 1662). Below are 47 quotes by Blaise Pascal from QuotePlace, each as a portrait card.

Thinker · 1623 – 1662

French mathematician, physicist and philosopher, author of the Pensées.

Quotes by Blaise Pascal

1.The heart has its reasons which reason knows nothing of.
2.All of humanity's problems stem from man's inability to sit quietly in a room alone.
3.The heart has its reasons which reason knows not of.
4.Kind words do not cost much. Yet they accomplish much.
5.Man is equally incapable of seeing the nothingness from which he emerges and the infinity in which he is engulfed.
6.Imagination disposes of everything; it creates beauty, justice, and happiness, which are everything in this world.
7.The least movement is of importance to all nature. The entire ocean is affected by a pebble.
8.We are all something, but none of us are everything.
9.We know the truth, not only by the reason, but by the heart.
10.We must learn our limits. We are all something, but none of us are everything.
11.Justice without force is powerless; force without justice is tyrannical.
12.Man's greatness lies in his power of thought.
13.We are generally the better persuaded by the reasons we discover ourselves than by those given to us by others.
14.Small minds are concerned with the extraordinary, great minds with the ordinary.
15.Man is neither angel nor brute, and the unfortunate thing is that he who would act the angel acts the brute.
16.Man is only a reed, the weakest in nature, but he is a thinking reed.
17.The eternal silence of these infinite spaces terrifies me.
18.Man is but a reed, the most feeble thing in nature, but he is a thinking reed.
19.The heart has its reasons which reason knows not.
20.We know the truth, not only by the reason, but also by the heart.
21.It is the heart which perceives God and not the reason.
22.Kind words do not cost much. They never blister the tongue or lips.
23.If all men knew what others say of them, there would not be four friends in the world.
24.The supreme function of reason is to show man that some things are beyond reason.
25.Thought constitutes the greatness of man.
26.It is not from space that I must seek my dignity, but from the government of my thought.
27.I have made this letter longer than usual because I lack the time to make it shorter.
28.Reason's last step is the recognition that there are an infinite number of things which are beyond it.
29.Do you wish people to think well of you? Don't speak well of yourself.
30.We never live, but we hope to live; and as we are always arranging to be happy, it is inevitable that we should never be so.
31.Men never do evil so completely and cheerfully as when they do it from religious conviction.
32.There are only two kinds of men: the righteous who think they are sinners and the sinners who think they are righteous.
33.In faith there is enough light for those who want to believe and enough shadows to blind those who don't.
34.The struggle alone pleases us, not the victory.
35.We never keep to the present. We recall the past; we anticipate the future as if we found it too slow in coming and were trying to hurry it up, or we recall the past as if to stay its too rapid flight.
36.The strength of a man's virtue must not be measured by his efforts, but by his ordinary life.
37.The greater intellect one has, the more originality one finds in men. Ordinary persons find no difference between men.
38.We do not content ourselves with the life we have in ourselves and in our own being; we desire to live an imaginary life in the mind of others, and for this purpose we endeavour to shine.
39.To make light of philosophy is to be a true philosopher.
40.People almost invariably arrive at their beliefs not on the basis of proof but on the basis of what they find attractive.
41.It is a natural illness of man to think that he possesses the truth directly.
42.All men seek happiness. This is without exception. Whatever different means they employ, they all tend to this end.
43.The knowledge of God is very far from the love of Him.
44.The last thing one settles in writing a book is what one should put in first.
45.We run carelessly to the precipice, after we have put something before us to prevent us seeing it.
46.What a chimera then is man! What a novelty! What a monster, what a chaos, what a contradiction, what a prodigy! Judge of all things, imbecile worm of the earth; depositary of truth, a sink of uncertainty and error; the pride and refuse of the universe!
47.We desire truth, and find within ourselves only uncertainty. We seek happiness, and find only misery and death.
