Quotes from Enchiridion

The Enchiridion — the 'handbook' — is Epictetus distilled: 53 short chapters compiled by his student Arrian from the Discourses. Its first line draws the distinction everything else follows from: some things are up to us, and some are not. Below: the 12 quotes in the QuotePlace collection that are verifiably from this work, each marked 'Source verified' on its own page.

Men are disturbed not by things, but by the view which they take of them.
Epictetus
It's not what happens to you, but how you react to it that matters.
Epictetus
Keep silence for the most part, and speak only when you must, and then briefly.
Epictetus
Men are disturbed, not by things, but by the principles and notions which they form concerning things.
Epictetus
Don't demand that things happen as you wish, but wish that they happen as they do happen, and you will go on well.
Epictetus
If you want to improve, be content to be thought foolish and stupid.
Epictetus
Do not seek to have events happen as you want them to, but instead want them to happen as they do happen, and your life will go well.
Epictetus
Do not say of anything that 'I have lost it,' but that 'I have given it back.'
Epictetus
Some things are within our power, and some things are not.
Epictetus
Men are disturbed not by things, but by the views which they take of them.
Epictetus
It is not what happens to you, but how you react to it that matters.
Epictetus
You may be always victorious if you will never enter into any contest where the issue does not depend on yourself.
Epictetus
