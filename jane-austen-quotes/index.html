English novelist known for Pride and Prejudice and Emma, celebrated for irony and social observation.

Quotes by Jane Austen

16.To be fond of dancing was a certain step towards falling in love.

18.Selfishness must always be forgiven, you know, because there is no hope of a cure.
19.Friendship is certainly the finest balm for the pangs of disappointed love.
20.She had been forced into prudence in her youth, she learned romance as she grew older: the natural sequel of an unnatural beginning.
21.Angry people are not always wise.
22.For what do we live, but to make sport for our neighbours, and laugh at them in our turn?
23.Know your own happiness. You want nothing but patience — or give it a more fascinating name, call it hope.
24.A lady's imagination is very rapid; it jumps from admiration to love, from love to matrimony, in a moment.
25.To sit in the shade on a fine day, and look upon verdure, is the most perfect refreshment.
26.I wish, as well as everybody else, to be perfectly happy; but, like everybody else, it must be in my own way.
27.You pierce my soul. I am half agony, half hope.
28.Silly things do cease to be silly if they are done by sensible people in an impudent way.
29.The more I know of the world, the more I am convinced that I shall never see a man whom I can really love. I require so much!
30.A large income is the best recipe for happiness I ever heard of.
31.Every man is surrounded by a neighbourhood of voluntary spies.
32.We have all a better guide in ourselves, if we would attend to it, than any other person can be.
33.Why not seize the pleasure at once? How often is happiness destroyed by preparation, foolish preparation!
34.A woman especially, if she have the misfortune of knowing anything, should conceal it as well as she can.
35.Surprises are foolish things. The pleasure is not enhanced, and the inconvenience is often considerable.
36.There are few people whom I really love, and still fewer of whom I think well. The more I see of the world, the more am I dissatisfied with it.
37.Seldom, very seldom, does complete truth belong to any human disclosure; seldom can it happen that something is not a little disguised, or a little mistaken.
38.One does not love a place the less for having suffered in it, unless it has been all suffering, nothing but suffering.
