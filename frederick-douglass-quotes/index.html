Frederick Douglass Quotes

Frederick Douglass (Abolitionist, writer, 1818 – 1895). Below are 25 quotes by Frederick Douglass from QuotePlace, each as a quote card.

Abolitionist, writer · 1818 – 1895

American abolitionist and writer who escaped slavery and became a leading voice against it.

Quotes by Frederick Douglass

1.If there is no struggle, there is no progress.
2.It is easier to build strong children than to repair broken men.
3.I would unite with anybody to do right and with nobody to do wrong.
4.I prefer to be true to myself, even at the hazard of incurring the ridicule of others, rather than to be false, and incur my own abhorrence.
5.Power concedes nothing without a demand. It never did and it never will.
6.The white man's happiness cannot be purchased by the black man's misery.
7.You have seen how a man was made a slave; you shall see how a slave was made a man.
8.No man can put a chain about the ankle of his fellow man without at last finding the other end fastened about his own neck.
9.For it is not light that is needed, but fire; it is not the gentle shower, but thunder. We need the storm, the whirlwind, and the earthquake.
10.The whole history of the progress of human liberty shows that all concessions yet made to her august claims have been born of earnest struggle.
11.The thing worse than rebellion is the thing that causes rebellion.
12.I have observed this in my experience of slavery, that whenever my condition was improved, instead of its increasing my contentment, it only increased my desire to be free, and set me to thinking of plans to gain my freedom.
13.From that moment, I understood the pathway from slavery to freedom.
14.I have found that, to make a contented slave, it is necessary to make a thoughtless one.
15.Where justice is denied, where poverty is enforced, where ignorance prevails, and where any one class is made to feel that society is an organized conspiracy to oppress, rob, and degrade them, neither persons nor property will be safe.
16.The soul that is within me no man can degrade.
17.Knowledge unfits a child to be a slave.
18.Those who profess to favor freedom and yet deprecate agitation are men who want crops without plowing up the ground.
19.The life of the nation is secure only while the nation is honest, truthful, and virtuous.
20.Find out just what any people will quietly submit to and you have found out the exact measure of injustice and wrong which will be imposed upon them, and these will continue till they are resisted with either words or blows, or with both.
21.Slaves are generally expected to sing as well as to work.
22.Every tone was a testimony against slavery, and a prayer to God for deliverance from chains.
23.To suppress free speech is a double wrong. It violates the rights of the hearer as well as those of the speaker.
24.I have often been asked, how I felt when first I found myself on free soil.
25.I recognize the Republican party as the sheet anchor of the colored man's political hopes and the ark of his safety.
