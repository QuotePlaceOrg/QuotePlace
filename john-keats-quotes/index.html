John Keats Quotes

John Keats (Poet, 1795 – 1821). Below are 40 quotes by John Keats from QuotePlace, each as a quote card.

Poet · 1795 – 1821

English Romantic poet of the great Odes, including Ode to a Nightingale.

Quotes by John Keats

1.A thing of beauty is a joy for ever.
2.Beauty is truth, truth beauty, — that is all ye know on earth, and all ye need to know.
3.A thing of beauty is a joy forever.
4.Heard melodies are sweet, but those unheard are sweeter.
5.I am certain of nothing but the holiness of the Heart's affections and the truth of Imagination.
6.My heart aches, and a drowsy numbness pains my sense.
7.Give me books, French wine, fruit, fine weather and a little music out of doors, played by somebody I do not know.
8.Much have I travelled in the realms of gold.
9.The poetry of earth is never dead.
10.Do you not see how necessary a world of pains and troubles is to school an intelligence and make it a soul?
11.I almost wish we were butterflies and liv'd but three summer days — three such days with you I could fill with more delight than fifty common years could ever contain.
12.Nothing ever becomes real till it is experienced.
13.I think I shall be among the English Poets after my death.
14.What the imagination seizes as Beauty must be truth — whether it existed before or not.
15.Where are the songs of Spring? Ay, where are they? Think not of them, thou hast thy music too.
16.Season of mists and mellow fruitfulness.
17.If Poetry comes not as naturally as the Leaves to a tree it had better not come at all.
18.A Poet is the most unpoetical of anything in existence; because he has no Identity—he is continually in for—and filling some other Body.
19.Love is my religion — I could die for that.
20.My imagination is a monastery and I am its monk.
21.Bright star, would I were steadfast as thou art.
22.O for a Life of Sensations rather than of Thoughts!
23.The setting sun will always set me to rights, or if a sparrow come before my window, I take part in its existence and pick about the gravel.
24.The excellence of every art is its intensity, capable of making all disagreeables evaporate, from their being in close relationship with Beauty and Truth.
25.The only means of strengthening one's intellect is to make up one's mind about nothing — to let the mind be a thoroughfare for all thoughts, not a select party.
26.When I have fears that I may cease to be before my pen has glean'd my teeming brain
27.Then felt I like some watcher of the skies when a new planet swims into his ken
28.I have loved the principle of beauty in all things, and if I had had time I would have made myself remembered.
29.The imagination may be compared to Adam's dream — he awoke and found it truth.
30.A man's life of any worth is a continual allegory — and very few eyes can see the mystery of life — a life like the Scriptures, figurative.
31.The genius of poetry must work out its own salvation in a man; it cannot be matured by law and precept, but by sensation and watchfulness in itself.
32.Here lies one whose name was writ in water.
33.Poetry should be great and unobtrusive, a thing which enters into one's soul, and does not startle it or amaze it with itself, but with its subject.
34.Scenery is fine — but human nature is finer.
35.Thou wast not born for death, immortal Bird!
36.Darkling I listen; and, for many a time, I have been half in love with easeful Death.
37.Or like stout Cortez when with eagle eyes he stared at the Pacific — and all his men looked at each other with a wild surmise — silent, upon a peak in Darien.
38.There is nothing stable in the world; uproar's your only music.
39.I have been astonished that men could die martyrs for their religion.
40.While we are laughing, the seed of trouble is put into the wide arable land of events.
