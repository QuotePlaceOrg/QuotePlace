Epictetus Quotes

Epictetus (Stoic philosopher, 50 – 135). Below are 52 quotes by Epictetus from QuotePlace, each as a beautiful quote card.

Stoic philosopher · 50 – 135

Epictetus was a Greek-born slave in Rome who won his freedom and became one of the most influential Stoic teachers. His Discourses and the handbook known as the Enchiridion teach that freedom lies in mastering one's own judgments and desires.

Quotes by Epictetus

1.First say to yourself what you would be; and then do what you have to do.
2.Wealth consists not in having great possessions, but in having few wants.
3.Freedom is the right to live as we wish.
4.Difficulties are things that show a person what they are.
5.If you wish to be a writer, write.

9.He is a wise man who does not grieve for the things which he has not, but rejoices for those which he has.

11.If you seek truth you will not seek victory by dishonourable means, and if you find truth you will become invincible.
12.When you are offended at any man's fault, turn to yourself and study your own failings. Then you will forget your anger.
13.Know, first, who you are, and then adorn yourself accordingly.
14.Men are disturbed not by things, but by the view which they take of them.

16.Not every difficult and dangerous thing is suitable for training, but only that which is conducive to success in achieving the object of our effort.

18.It's not what happens to you, but how you react to it that matters.

21.It is impossible for a man to learn what he thinks he already knows.
22.One that desires to excel should endeavour in those things that are in themselves most excellent.
23.Men are disturbed, not by things, but by the principles and notions which they form concerning things.
24.Don't demand that things happen as you wish, but wish that they happen as they do happen, and you will go on well.
25.Practice yourself, for heaven's sake, in little things; from them proceed to greater.
26.Any person capable of angering you becomes your master; he can anger you only when you permit yourself to be disturbed by him.
27.No great thing is created suddenly.
28.First learn the meaning of what you say, and then speak.
29.If you want to improve, be content to be thought foolish and stupid.
30.The greater the difficulty, the more glory in surmounting it. Skillful pilots gain their reputation from storms and tempests.
31.Whoever does not regard what he has as most ample wealth, is unhappy, though he be master of the world.
32.Don't explain your philosophy. Embody it.
33.Do not seek to have events happen as you want them to, but instead want them to happen as they do happen, and your life will go well.
34.Do not say of anything that 'I have lost it,' but that 'I have given it back.'
35.Circumstances don't make the man, they only reveal him to himself.
36.Control thy passions lest they take vengeance on thee.
37.It is not death or pain that is to be feared, but the fear of pain or death.
38.Some things are within our power, and some things are not.
39.Men are disturbed not by things, but by the views which they take of them.
40.Only the educated are free.
41.The greater the difficulty, the more glory in surmounting it.
42.Attach yourself to what is spiritually superior, regardless of what other people think or do.
43.Freedom is the only worthy goal in life. It is won by disregarding things that lie beyond our control.
44.Circumstances do not make the man, they only reveal him to himself.
45.Do not explain your philosophy. Embody it.
46.It is not what happens to you, but how you react to it that matters.
47.Man is not worried by real problems so much as by his imagined anxieties about real problems.
48.Nothing great is created suddenly, any more than a bunch of grapes or a fig. If you tell me that you desire a fig, I answer you that there must be time.
49.You may be always victorious if you will never enter into any contest where the issue does not depend on yourself.
50.He who laughs at himself never runs out of things to laugh at.
51.The key is to keep company only with people who uplift you, whose presence calls forth your best.
52.Freedom is secured not by the fulfilling of one's desires, but by the removal of desire.
