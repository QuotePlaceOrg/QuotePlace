Publilius Syrus Quotes

Publilius Syrus (Latin writer of maxims, 85 BC – 43 BC). Below are 43 quotes by Publilius Syrus from QuotePlace, each as a quote card.

Latin writer of maxims · 85 BC – 43 BC

Publilius Syrus was a Latin writer of the 1st century BC who was brought to Italy as a slave from Syria and later freed. He is known for his Sententiae, a collection of moral maxims drawn from his mimes.

Quotes by Publilius Syrus

1.A rolling stone gathers no moss.
2.While we stop to think, we often miss our opportunity.
3.Better be ignorant of a matter than half know it.
4.I have often regretted my speech, never my silence.
5.Do not turn back when you are just at the goal.
6.Never promise more than you can perform.
7.Anyone can hold the helm when the sea is calm.
8.We are interested in others when they are interested in us.
9.Fortune is like glass—the brighter the glitter, the more easily broken.
10.Practice is the best of all instructors.
11.No one knows what he can do till he tries.
12.It is a bad plan that admits of no modification.
13.A good reputation is more valuable than money.
14.From the errors of others, a wise man corrects his own.
15.When two do the same thing, it is not the same thing after all.
16.Let a fool hold his tongue and he will pass for a sage.
17.Audacity augments courage; hesitation, fear.
18.He doubly benefits the needy who gives quickly.
19.Poverty is the lack of many things, but avarice is the lack of all things.
20.Patience is a remedy for every sorrow.
21.Many receive advice, few profit by it.
22.Learn to see in another's calamity the ills which you should avoid.
23.He who helps the guilty, shares the crime.
24.Opportunity is lost by deliberation.
25.It is easier to do an injury than to bear one.
26.A wise man rules his passions, a fool obeys them.
27.The loss which is unknown is no loss at all.
28.Nothing can be done at once hastily and prudently.
29.The fear of death is more to be dreaded than death itself.
30.It is only the ignorant who despise education.
31.We desire nothing so much as what we ought not to have.
32.No man is happy who does not think himself so.
33.The judge is condemned, when the criminal is acquitted.
34.It is a universal law which ordains birth and death.
35.To forget the wrongs you receive, is to remedy them.
36.He invites crime who ignores sin.
37.No tears are shed, when an enemy dies.
38.Bitter for a free man is the bondage of debt.
39.Adversity shows whether we have friends, or only the shadows of friends.
40.Whom Fortune wishes to destroy she first makes mad.
41.When Fortune flatters, she does it to betray.
42.Confession of our faults is the next thing to innocence.
43.Necessity gives the law without itself acknowledging one.
