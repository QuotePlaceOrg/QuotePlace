Voltaire Quotes

Voltaire (French Enlightenment writer and philosopher, 1694 – 1778). Below are 27 quotes by Voltaire from QuotePlace, each as a text quote card.

French Enlightenment writer and philosopher · 1694 – 1778

Voltaire, born François-Marie Arouet, was a French Enlightenment writer, philosopher and historian famous for the novella Candide and for his witty defence of civil liberties, including freedom of speech and religion. He was one of the most prolific and influential writers of his age.

Quotes by Voltaire

1.No snowflake in an avalanche ever feels responsible.
2.To enjoy life, we must touch much of it lightly.
3.Think for yourselves and let others enjoy the privilege to do so too.
4.The longer we dwell on our misfortunes, the greater is their power to harm us.
5.We never live; we are always in the expectation of living.
6.Judge a man by his questions rather than by his answers.
7.It is dangerous to be right in matters on which the established authorities are wrong.
8.Let us read, and let us dance; these two amusements will never do any harm to the world.
9.God is a comedian playing to an audience too afraid to laugh.
10.The more I read, the more I acquire, the more certain I am that I know nothing.
11.Those who can make you believe absurdities can make you commit atrocities.
12.Common sense is not so common.
13.We must cultivate our garden.
14.Work keeps at bay three great evils: boredom, vice, and need.
15.Every man is guilty of all the good he did not do.
16.Doubt is not a pleasant condition, but certainty is absurd.
17.It is better to risk sparing a guilty person than to condemn an innocent one.
18.Let us work without theorizing; 'tis the only way to make life endurable.
19.Love truth, but pardon error.
20.If God did not exist, it would be necessary to invent him.
21.The best is the enemy of the good.
22.What is tolerance? It is the consequence of humanity. We are all formed of frailty and error; let us pardon reciprocally each other's folly — that is the first law of nature.
23.Superstition is to religion what astrology is to astronomy, the mad daughter of a wise mother.
24.Prejudice is opinion without judgement.
25.Man is free at the instant he wants to be.
26.What we find in books is like the fire in our hearths. We fetch it from our neighbors, we kindle it at home, we communicate it to others, and it becomes the property of all.
27.Where there is friendship, there is our natural soil.
